Six members of the regular faculty of the University of Virginia met on Saturday, December 8, 1855. Faculty chairman Socrates Maupin convened the meeting to discuss a formal complaint submitted by twenty-nine students who had subscribed to McCoy’s Hotel. The students solicited assistance from the faculty to improve the condition of their accommodations.

The students’ grievances focused largely on the condition of the fare served at McCoy’s table. Of particular concern were the biscuits, rolls, and corn bread which were “either very much burnt, or not cooked at all” and coffee and tea which were “not fit to drink.” The students were also distressed by the “perceptibly watered” milk served alongside “very tough” beef that was served “for dinner one day” only to “frequently make its appearance the next morning in the form of steak or hash.” In addition, the students found “the vegetables” to be “deficient in both quantity and quality” and the dessert “wanting in variety and proper preparation.” 

In addition to those epicurean complaints, the students claimed that McCoy’s enslaved servants were “utterly regardless of the wants of students at meals” and served food that “is decidedly unclean.” This episode reveals a common refrain at UVA before 1865—most complaints from white students or faculty member would blame enslaved laborers for the problem, whatever it might be. 

Student complaints about dining hall food while away at college are apparently timeless, but this episode reveals some important details about life at the early University. First, hotel keepers struggled not only to get sufficient supplies to feed their students, and that problem was endemic at UVA for decades. Second, enslaved people found themselves overworked at UVA hotels, and students routinely blamed enslaved people for any and all problems.

After reading the written complaint, the faculty heard testimony from six University students, Socrates Maupin, and the University Proctor. Mr. McCoy was also offered the opportunity to present a defense. After discussing all of the testimony and the report, the faculty adopted a preamble and resolution regarding the incident. The preamble stated that the faculty found “just grounds of complaint of Mr. McCoy’s table in the particulars of the biscuits and attention of the \[enslaved] servants.” As a result, Mr. McCoy was “admonished” and required to prove “amendment in these particulars” to the Faculty. 

Even the faculty, in response to a student complaint about food quality in a single hotel, made sure in their report to blame some of the problems on the enslaved people themselves who were forced to cook the food, serve the students, and also clean up after meals while cleaning student dormitories.
