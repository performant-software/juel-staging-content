On the morning of January 26, 1834, students William P. Braxton and John M. Forbes made a highly unusual demand to their hotelkeeper. They asked to be served their breakfast in their dormitories, not in John Rose's dining hall. Rose, their hotelkeeper, refused the request, but he did send an unidentified enslaved domestic servant, to fix up the beds, likely in hopes of mollifying Forbes and Braxton.

Forbes, insulted by Rose's refusal to provide them special room service, lashed out. He complained that enslaved servants were incompetent and then physically assaulted the enslaved man sent to make the students' beds. Forbes kicked him, tossed him out their room, and reported to Mr. Rose that he \[Forbes] would be forced to not only reeport the hotelkeeper to UVA, but also to personally whip the hotelkeeper. The threat of whipping made clear that Forbes was exercising his masterly power and saw both the enslaved and the hotelkeeper as his inferiors. The crisis passed when Mr. Forbes and Mr. Braxton chose to go into town to have breakfast at Keller’s Confectionary.

The faculty, charged with student discipline, decided Forbes' conduct was inappropriate and chose to suspend both Forbes and Braxton for two weeks. Their crime, however, was not assaulting an enslaved man, nor threatening to whip a UVA hotelkeeper. Remarkably, the faculty punished them only for leaving UVA Grounds to go to a known drinking house.
