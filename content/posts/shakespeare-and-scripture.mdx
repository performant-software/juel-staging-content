Students at the University of Virginia frequently turned to literature when signing their classmates' autograph books. Autograph books were popular at colleges and universities in the nineteenth century. They were typically printed locally, included lithographs and etchings of the school interspersed with blank pages. They were, in a way, the predecessor of the collegiate yearbook. Students would have friends add notes and doodles to their books as a keepsake from their time in school.  

Although students would quote Alexander Pope or John Milton here and there, the two most common sources of inspiration for UVA students were William Shakespeare and the Bible, specifically the Book of Proverbs. Sometimes misquoted or taken out of context, these quotations are still an interesting glimpse into the common wisdom of the time.

The quotations below are copied as they were written in UVA student autograph books (they included spelling errors and copy mistakes as they appeared in the original handwritten notes).

Quotations from the Book of Proverbs:

"Look not when the wine when it is red - when it giveth its color in the cup - for at last it biteth like a serpent and stingeth like an adder!" **23:32**

"Say unto wisdom. Thou art my sister; and call understanding thy kinswoman" **7:4**

"Surely the churning of the milk bringeth forth butter, and the wringing of the nose bringeth forth blood, so the forcing wrath surrendereth strife" **30:33**

Quotations from Shakespeare:

Dispute it like a man

I shall do so:

But I must also feel it like a man:

I cannot but remember things were

That were most pleasant to me

**Macbeth, Act IV Scene iii**

'To thine own self be true, and it shall follow, as the day the night that thou canst not then be false to any man"

**Hamlet, Act I Scene iii**

“I count myself in nothing else so happy as in a soul remembering my good friends”

**Richard II, Act II Scene iii**\*

The Shakespeare quotes in particular seem fitting—the young male students had bonded as friends in their time at UVA. Those were likely the first years any of them had spent considerable time away from home and they were also key years in student identity formation as young adults. 

The prevalence of scriptural quotations also reveals the shared Protestant religious culture shared by almost all UVA students. They had grown up attending church with their families and continued as co-religionists at UVA, either in services conducted by the UVA Chaplain or by joining the congregation of one of the local Protestant churches. 

The autograph books, while not deeply revealing of the interior lives or experiences of students, do reveal quite a bit about shared student culture at UVA before 1865. 

\*This quotation was repeated in multiple autograph books by different signers.
