In the early decades after UVA opened to its first class of students, maintaining the functionality and cleanliness of buildings proved a continual challenge. In no case was this more apparent than with the University’s privies (outhouses). These spaces, hardly the glorified or carefully planned temples of the Lawn Pavilions or Rotunda, were literally necessaries—every inhabitant of the university from students to enslaved people, to faculty and their families, used these outdoor toilets daily. They required frequent cleaning, as they were simply small buildings over open waste pits. The pits filled up quickly and sometimes overlflowed. Their cleaning was often either neglected or was delayed by other work. For students, they often also became the site of foul pranks. AlthoughJefferson went to great lengths to detail the construction of his formal public spaces, he often provided little in the way of instructions about placement, use, or management of outbuildings. this was in part because he considered some of them private spaces to be tailored by individual residents, and in part because his designs evinced little interest in how people would actually live in and use non-public spaces.[\[1\]](#_ftn1)

The expense of maintaining the Garden privies belonged to the professors and Hotel Keepers who occupied the adjacent pavilions and hotels, but the role of managing their maintenance them fell to the Proctor.[\[2\]](#_ftn2) The actual labor of cleaning out the waste pits and cleaning the privy buildings fell to enslaved people. Rest assured this was no easy job. 

In an 1849 review of the state of Grounds, the faculty  graphically described their function and their condition:

*"The privies used for the accommodation of the students were found to be in as good condition as the proverbially careless habits of the persons using them and the extremely faulty construction of the buildings rendered possible. They are cleaned daily, lime being thrown liberally into the boxes and sprinkled on the floors, but as the sleepers of the floor rut directly upon the ground, a necessary consequence is that animal effluvia have been generated abundantly in the very small space between the floor and the soil and that there are no means of removing them and ventilating the undersurface of the floor."*

Lime, used as a universal sanitizing product at the early University, was clearly proving insufficient in its ability to sanitize and mask odors around the outbuildings. To deal with these conditions, the faculty suggested raising the privies about two feet off the ground to improve ventilation.[\[3\]](#_ftn3)

Those often overflowing privy waste pits and poor drainage systems were often blamed for bad health conditions around Grounds. In November 1825, a bout of fever and illness traveled around Spotswood’s hotel. The faculty presumed it was because the nearby drains were “choked with vegetable and animal substance.”[\[4\]](#_ftn4) Through the first several decades of operation, the Board of Visitors agreed that the “public water closets…\[were] endangering the health of the students.”[\[5\]](#_ftn5)

At other times privies needed to be moved, rebuilt or new ones built from scratch. Occasionally, a more disastrous fate might befall a privy, as students damaged them in sometimes spectacular ways. In December 1829, for example, unidentified student set fire to two University privies, one near Mr. Rose’s Hotel (Hotel F) and the other near the Anatomical Theater (west of Hotel A). For several days the Chairman and investigators tried to track down the culprits. A week later, the proctor responded by asking the Board of Visitors for funds to construct new privies.[\[6\]](#_ftn6)

The use of outdoor privies faded slowly over the second half of the nineteenth century, perhaps reflecting a general change in the use of garden and outdoor spaces following the Civil War, but also reflecting modernization at the school. A pavilion resident requested the first indoor restroom in 1876. Similar requests as late as the 1890s suggest institutional practices regarding outhouses were slow to develop at the University.[\[7\]](#_ftn7)
