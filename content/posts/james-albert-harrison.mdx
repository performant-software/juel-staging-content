James Albert Harrison was born in Pass Christian, Mississippi, on August 24, 1848. He grew up in New Orleans, Louisiana, and died in Charlottesville, Virginia in January 1911.  He was the son of Jilson Payne Harrison and Sidney Ann Powell Norton. Harrison first came to the University of Virginia as a student in 1866. He studied Modern Languages, German, Latin, Chemistry, and Moral Philosophy. Harrison would eventually return to the University in 1895 as professor of Romantic Languages and Literature. He was paid a salary of $3,300 and lived in Pavilion II.  Before coming to the University as a professor, Harrison studied in Germany at the University of Bonn and taught Modern Languages at both Randolph-Macon College and Washington and Lee University.

An accomplished scholar and author, Harrison received honorary degrees from Randolph-Macon College, Washington and Lee University, Tulane University, and Columbia University.  His scholarly works include editions of *Beowulf*, the *A Handy Poetical Anglo-Saxon Dictionary*, *An Anglo-Saxon Prose Reader for Beginners in Oldest English* (co-edited with William Malone Baskervil), a biography of George Washingon (*George Washington: Patriot, Soldier, Statesman), The Story of Greece, *the* Life of Edgar Allen Poe,*and a seventeen-volume edited collection, *The Complete Works of Edgar Allen Poe*.* *During his time as a UVA Professor, Harrison wrote an [essay](http://juel.iath.virginia.edu/node/343?doc=/juel_display/periodicals/AlumniBulletin/JamesAHarrison-on-UVaCemetery) on the history of the university cemetery. The essay poetically describes the landscape around, within, and surrounding the cemetery, as well as providing two letters from “authentic lips” that describe the creation of the cemetery.
