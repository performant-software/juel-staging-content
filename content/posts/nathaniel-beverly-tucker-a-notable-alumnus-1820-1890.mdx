Beverly N. Tucker (born June 8, 1820) was a first-year student at the University during the 1837-1838 session. Tucker requested to matriculate in 1836 but, along with several other students, faced difficulties when he failed to bring a certificate from his prior education or other “satisfactory evidence of good character.” However, he was able to enter the University in 1837, studying Mathematics, Natural Philosophy, and Moral Philosophy.

Prior to his education at UVA, Tucker had attended a private classical and mathematical school started in 1835 by Socrates Maupin, a UVA alumnus (MD 1830, MA 1833, and UVA professor 1853-1870). *The Richmond Whig* predicted its success: “Every father in the city, ought to feel a particular and individual interest in its welfare: and to promote that, should remember that the City, through the agency of its public opinion, more than the professors themselves, will controul its character.” Interestingly enough, it seems both Maupin's school and University at the time faced difficulties in taming the rowdy nature of their young male students. In that regard, Beverly Tucker was a typical student, first in Richmond under Maupin's direction. At UVA, he was reprimanded by the faculty on several occasions over the course of a single academic year, for instances such as: hosting a festival entertainment (a party), intoxication at a student ball, and violating the “law regarding early rising.” The University wrote to his father regarding his violations and even suspended him for a time at one point during the year as punishment.

Thus, Tucker was a typical pre-1870 UVA student in several respects. First, he had been raised to mastery, refusing to take orders from anyone. Second, as a young adult free from his parents for the first time, he avidly pursued social activities. Last, he and many other students annually before 1870 remained less than committed to academics and did not complete degrees. Within a year, Beverly N. Tucker had discontinued his studies at UVA and largely disappears from the record.
