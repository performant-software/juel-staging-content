Enoch Faw came to study law at UVA in 1857.  He was from Davidson, North Carolina and had previously attended Normal College from 1851 to 1856, North Carolina's first state teacher's college (it would later become Trinity College and then Duke University). Faw next spent a year in Marietta, Georgia, studying law under General A.J. Hansell, before coming to UVA. Normal College President, Braxton Craven, wrote Faw's letter of recommendation for UVA. 

Faw arrived in Charlottesville in fall 1857 as a new student. It was Faw's first time traveling out of his home state. In his free time, he took in all the Virginia Piedmont had to offer, including a visit to “far famed,” Monticello, Thomas Jefferson's home. Jefferson of course had died over thirty years earlier. Monticello at the time was owned by Commodore (U.S. Navy) Uriah Phillips Levy, who used the property primarily as a summer vacation home and private residence. Levy employed Albemarle resident Joel Wheeler as the property overseer, who managed both free and enslaved laborers who maintained the house and working plantation.

Faw was quite taken with Monticello and the views it offered: “The scenery is very good from there. Charlottesville lies in the N.W. beautifully spread out in the plain, while a great number of white houses may be seen scattered in the sunshine and green foliage over the red hills lying between the ranges of little mountains… The place itself is one of grandeur.”

Faw also visited Jefferson’s grave: “The monument over his grave is but a square block of granit \[sic], perhaps 2 feet cube with rather apyramidal stone about 5 ½ high resting upon the cube.”  He commented that the “old and dilapidated,” appearance is a result of visitors taking pieces from the monument as souvenirs, “through a spirit of veneration for the man.”  Even in 1857, the reverence of Thomas Jefferson was thriving.

Faw's diary also provides a glimpse into student life at UVA in the late 1850s. He commented on the friendliness of the students and related his “utter despair” over complicated Common Law abstracts. One of his trips to town involved purchasing $73 worth of textbooks, which reveals just how costly it was to attend the school at the time. Faw would withdraw from UVA on June 2, 1858, less than a year after coming to Charlottesville. 

After his time at the University, Enoch Faw returned to Georgia. He would fight in the Civil War, enlisting in the Confederate army as 1st lieutenant in Company D, 4th Reserves Infantry Regiment, Georgia.  After the war, he moved back to Marietta, Georgia, where he was elected mayor in 1884.  He continued to practice law in Marietta until his death in 1902.
